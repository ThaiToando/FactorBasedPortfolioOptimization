Factor structure versus direct estimation

A reproduction of Auh & Cho (2023)  ·  Economics Letters 228, 111137

Abstract. The contribution of Auh and Cho (2023) is a change in what one estimates. Direct estimation fits N expected returns, each from a short and noisy sample. The factor approach fits a single market premium and projects it through betas, which are persistent and estimated far more precisely. This note runs both methods on the same synthetic universe out of sample. As the number of assets grows or the estimation window shortens, direct estimation degrades while the factor approach holds.

1.Discussion

The parameter count is the whole story. Direct estimation fits N free means; the factor model fits one premium and N betas, but betas are far easier to estimate than means (Merton, 1980), so the effective noise is much lower. The empirical reproduction found the factor strategies earning roughly three percent annual alpha at one-fifth the concentration of direct estimation — the empirical shadow of this simulation.
