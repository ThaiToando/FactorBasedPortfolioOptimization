Estimation error and the corner solution

A reproduction of Auh & Cho (2023)  ·  Economics Letters 228, 111137

Abstract. Mean–variance optimization requires expected returns as input, and estimating them from historical means injects noise of the same order as the means themselves (Merton, 1980). This note makes the consequence visible. As estimation noise rises, the long-only maximum-Sharpe optimizer abandons diversification and allocates to whichever asset’s error happened to look most attractive — the corner solution that the factor structure of Auh and Cho (2023) is designed to avoid.

1.Discussion

The believed Sharpe is what the optimizer reports using its noisy estimate of μ; the realised Sharpe is what the chosen weights earn on the true means. Their divergence is the cost of treating an estimate as truth. The factor approach, μi = rf + βi π, replaces the N noisy means with a single premium π and N persistent betas, and so does not corner.
